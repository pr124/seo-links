## Patent and trial evidence

The [information-gain patent, US11354342B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11354342B2/en), records filing on 18 October 2018 and grant on 7 June 2022. It describes additional information relative to documents previously viewed by a user. A patent describes an invention; it does not establish that Google currently deploys it in ordinary search rankings, nor which weights or implementations are used.

The [DOJ-hosted Nayak exhibit](https://www.justice.gov/atr/media/1398866/dl) describes NavBoost using query/document clicks segmented by location and device, with the most recent 13 months of data. A separate [Lehman exhibit](https://www.justice.gov/atr/media/1398886/dl) describes a click table and cautions against calling that table a machine-learning system. These are historical trial records, not a guaranteed current retention specification or a site-owner ranking recipe.

The supplied names originalContentScore, titlematchScore, bylineDate, syntacticDate, lastSignificantUpdate, contentage, goodClicks, badClicks, lastLongestClicks and siteAuthority are retained as source claims in the structured resource record. Their exact operational definitions, weights and current usage were not validated here. No last-click target, dwell-time threshold, mandatory history length or “cannot fake it” guarantee is inferred from the names.

## Google Sites / parasite-SEO source

The supplied September 24 workflow covers six steps: keyword research, a simple Google Sites page, naturally relevant content, an offer link, publication and a ranking claim. The October 5 fragment supplies the repeated final step. The basic workflow duplicates guidance already in the repo, so it is retained as recovered source context rather than a new recommendation.

The first-page ranking promise, $1,000/month outcome and faster Google indexing from social sharing were not supported by reviewed evidence. Hosting on Google Sites does not itself establish a ranking advantage. The video and claimed real-world outcome were not inspected.

[Google’s current spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) distinguish third-party content from content placed mainly to exploit a host’s established ranking signals. Third-party publishing alone is not a violation. The same policies cover keyword stuffing and link spam; a platform’s high authority does not make every placement appropriate.

The complete supplied Musicfy article is relevant to product-led SaaS SEO. Its distinctive useful advice is to map a real feature/use-case inventory to commercial searches, build structured pages with meaningful demonstrations, and connect those pages to supporting guides. These points now appear as a compact workflow in the guide. Overlapping advice about clear positioning, comparisons and direct answers is associated with the existing AI-content entry.

Commercial affiliate links need appropriate qualification; the article is not proof of ranking credit from paid placements.

## Curation rule for existing and future posts

Keep a post when readable content supplies a useful, distinct SEO method or reference. Add a destination only if it is relevant and absent from the whole repo. Attach repeated source mentions to its existing entry. A newly supplied submission route or checked condition can enrich that entry without creating a duplicate.

Use an article title, its original URL and a brief takeaway. Unreadable titles, pure promotions, unsupported ranking/revenue guarantees and ambiguous destinations stay in the source register until they add something usable. Preserve original authors, dates and positions; never turn an incomplete thread into a complete checklist.

## Calculator and translation review

Calculator pages should record formula provenance and assumptions, with checks for units, rounding, known results, boundary values and invalid inputs. Review explanations alongside computed outputs. Health-related calculations need qualified domain review; publishing a medication-related calculator is not validated by its impressions or clicks. This guide supplies a quality checklist, not medical formulas or dosing advice.

Translated pages need their own terminology, unit, example and disclosure review. Check equivalent-version links and language targeting; do not infer a benefit merely from publishing more languages.

## Observation versus attribution

Repeat AI-search tests with a consistent query set and documented conditions. Citation changes do not establish their cause. Compare landing-page outcomes with search data using clear attribution and date ranges; clicks and sessions are different measurements. SMO and the supplied author's DEO label describe activities or frameworks, not new documented ranking factors.
